This scenario describes a common e-commerce strategy. You're essentially acting as a middleman, sourcing products at a lower cost and selling them at a higher price to generate profit. For instance, purchasing an item for $3 from AliExpress and then listing it on Amazon or eBay for $10 means you're aiming for a $7 gross profit per item. After accounting for the platform's fees (which can vary, but let's say they take $2 on eBay), your net profit is $5. If you then decide to split this $5 profit 50/50 with a partner or reinvest half, you'd each get $2.50 per sale. This model relies on efficient sourcing, accurate pricing to cover all costs and fees, and effective marketing to attract buyers to your listings. The key is finding products with a sufficient price difference between sourcing and selling points to make the effort worthwhile.
